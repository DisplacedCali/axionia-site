# Axionia — site review, 27 August 2026

Pass over the public marketing surface only: `app/page.tsx`, `/platform`,
`/deck`, and the components they pull. Written to be worked through, not filed.
Ordered by reversibility rather than by impact — see *How to work this* below.

**Headline:** the site argues well and it argues three times. The home page runs
~1,940 words over eleven sections making essentially one case (*nobody
independently checked this*) at three levels of abstraction: a diagnosis, an
arithmetic, and a set of values. Each is well written; together they queue
rather than compound. The larger miss is that nothing on the home page says
what Axionia is **instead of** — every claim is made against an unnamed
"chain", so a reader already carrying Springbuk, or reading an Artemis Console
scorecard, or three years into a Mercer contract cannot tell whether we replace
them, sit beside them, or duplicate them.

One accuracy problem is separated out as C1 and should be treated as more than
a copy tidy: the site currently asserts clinical overlap between programs,
which is not something we can know without claims data. See below.

---

## How to work this

**One file-coherent change, one commit, one `COMMIT_MSG.txt`.** The natural unit
is a finding, but where several findings touch the same file they ship together
— four separate commits against `app/page.tsx` would need interactive staging,
which the sandbox can't do. Track A is therefore one commit, not four.

**Order by reversibility, not by impact.** Deletions first: "wrong" costs
nothing and the revert is exact. Then relocations, where content moves and
nothing is rewritten. Then merges, which need new prose. Then new components.
The differentiator section (D2) is the biggest miss on the site and is
deliberately last — writing its copy against a page that is about to lose four
sections means writing it twice.

**Mechanical and editorial are different jobs.** Deleting a duplicated sentence
doesn't touch voice. Merging two sections does. Editorial items arrive as
copy-only drafts with no code attached, and become commits after Tom has edited
them. Never more than one editorial item in flight.

**Gates, by owner.** `npx tsc --noEmit` is the agent's and runs in the sandbox.
Reading the diff is Tom's. `npm run build` and looking at the page is Tom's —
the build does not finish inside the sandbox command timeout.

## Track C — editorial

New prose required. Drafted as copy first, edited by Tom, then committed.

**C1 — the site claims overlap it cannot know. It means double-counted value.**
This is an accuracy item, not a copy tidy, and it is upstream of C2.

`BenefitOverlap.tsx` is already correct and its header comment is more precise
than any copy around it. The math is the independent-overlap union of *claims
made against a pool of avoidable spend* —
`dedup = pool × (1 − Π(1 − claimᵢ / pool))` — deliberately assuming programs
overlap only as much as chance would predict, stated as a floor so the number
survives a vendor pushing back. The comment says the defect plainly: *no one
owns the union.*

Everything visible says something else and something we cannot support:

- the component name `BenefitOverlap`
- the eyebrow "The savings nobody de-duplicates"
- the headline "Five programs. Five sets of savings. One population."
- `REVIEW_GAPS[1]` — "whether the fourth one overlaps the first three"

All four assert that the *programs* overlap, which is a clinical claim about
care delivery that we have no data to make and that CLAUDE.md's
never-invent-library-data rule should have caught. The defensible claim is
narrower and harder to argue with: **five attribution models are pointed at one
pool of avoidable spend, so some of that value is being counted more than once,
and nobody owns the union.**

Vocabulary settled 2026-08-27: **double-counted value**. Framing rule carries
over unchanged — nobody is lying, each vendor reports its own program correctly,
and the duplication is a property of owning all five at once. The distinction
to hold in the copy is that vendors are not double-*charging*; the value is
being double-*counted*.

*Fix: rename the component, rewrite the four labels above, and reword
`REVIEW_GAPS[1]` so it describes attribution rather than care.*

**C2 — the stack and the double-count are one section wearing two hats.**
*A dollar here. Six dollars there.* and the double-counting section are the same
population from two sides; the code comment on the second says so. They run as
two full-height sections with ~260 words of setup between them, and they sit on
adjacent `bg-base-2` blocks (`:262`, `:293`) with no rule, so they already merge
visually without the benefit of being edited together. *Fix: one section, two
views, one setup — what you pay, then what you're actually getting.*

**C3 — trim *In plain terms*. Applied 2026-08-27, smaller than specified.**
B3 had already taken the fourth paragraph, and cutting to two would have meant
merging the audit half into the design half — new sentences, and PROJECT_STATE
is explicit that having both in that order is the product.

What was actually wrong was narrower: paragraph three was a trailer for a
section 250 lines below it. It promised "we tell you what else the same money
could buy"; the later section is titled "Knowing what it's worth is the start.
Knowing what else it could buy is the point." It also restated the incumbency
claim that the later pull quote makes better. The restatement is gone and the
promise stays, along with the no-catalogue line, which A1 deliberately kept
here as the place a first-time reader meets it.

## Track D — new build

**D1 — make the home page radar something the visitor touches.**
`RadarPreview` is static and illustrative. It is also the only place on the home
page where the product is visible. *This is where the primary CTA should live.*

**D2 — "What this replaces, and what it doesn't".**
The biggest miss on the site. A table by category — brokers and consultants,
vendor-paid validation, claims analytics, actuarial firms, independent
assessment — sorted on four axes: who pays, before or after purchase, one
program or the whole stack, category or your population. Exactly one cell is
empty and we are in it: employer-paid, pre-purchase, portfolio-level.

Keep the honest column. Milliman markets independent third-party review of
vendor ROI methodology directly, and PHTI is a citation rather than a
competitor. Being generous about what Artemis Console and Milliman are good at
buys more credibility than a clean sweep.

Related, and it should not survive this pass: the buyer deck's claim that the
independent analysis a CFO needs *"has never existed. Until now."* The true
version is stronger — Milliman will do this for a jumbo employer at six figures,
one program at a time; nobody does it for the 900-person manufacturer, and
nobody untangles double-counted value across a whole stack at any price.

A `/compare` page carries the named detail, and is where the search traffic is:
people look for "Springbuk alternative", not "benefit decision intelligence".

**D3 — let the ReportDemo visitor type the vendor's number. Done
2026-08-27, and it was not one state variable.**

The demo computed its expected case as `VENDOR_CLAIM × transfer × (1 − overlap)
× (1 − selection) × engagement/baseline`, so the model's independent answer was
derived from the number it exists to check. Because the standard deviation was
also a fixed multiple of the expected case, the claim term cancelled in the
percentile: $180, $90 and $400 all returned the same reading. An input alone
would have shipped a control that visibly did nothing.

The constant is now split. `CATEGORY_BASELINE` is what the category plausibly
returns for this population, modeled independently; `claim` is a pure input
that positions the marker, the percentile and the verdict. At 180 the baseline
reproduces the $54 expected case the rest of the site quotes, so the default
view did not change.

Open: `CATEGORY_BASELINE` should vary by segment and currently does not. This
became Track F — see `docs/EXPOSURE_MODEL.md`. It ships as one illustrative
constant rather than four invented ones until that lands, per CLAUDE.md.

**D4 — the double-count dial. Done 2026-08-27, and it was half-built.**

The model already carried `const OVERLAP = 0.2` — a fixed, invisible
duplication haircut with the vocabulary we removed everywhere else. It is now
`duplicationShare(n)`, the same union as `DoubleCountedValue`, driven by a
1-to-8 stepper. Four programs returns 20.3%, so the default output is
unchanged; the visitor now owns the number rather than having it applied to
them silently.

This is the only control in the demo that no competitor in E1's landscape can
offer, because every one of them evaluates a single program at a time.

**D5 — carry the demo configuration into /request-report.**
A visitor who has set headcount, profile, engagement and a vendor claim has just
supplied everything the intake asks for, and then hits a dead end.

**D6 — the illustrative line DeckFlow already carries. Done 2026-08-27.**
One line at the foot, separating what responds to the visitor's inputs from
what is illustrative underneath it.

## Track E — the illustrative report

`axionia_illustrative_report.html` — Northrock Industrial, added to project
knowledge 2026-08-27. A full worked Insight Report on a synthetic employer,

asset the company has and nothing else in the category publishes anything like

**E1 — three scoring sets, one name. Settled 2026-08-27.**

There were never two sets. There are three, and the one in production appears
nowhere in the marketing.

| Set | Axes | Object | Where it lives today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Evidence, Population fit, Cost efficiency, Utilization, Vendor stability, Engagement, Contract terms, Transparency | **Program** — each describes one vendor | Illustrative constants in `RadarPreview` and `ReportDemo`. Not in the database. Not produced by any pipeline. |
| **B** | spendEfficiency, decisionMaturity, workforceAlignment, vendorIndependence, analyticsReadiness, cfoEngagement, regulatoryReadiness, appreciationValue | **Company** | `lib/modules/research/pipeline/prompts.ts`, columns in `research_schema.sql`, report JSON shape in migration 010. **This is what a real run computes.** |
| **C** | Value verification, Economic alignment, Attribution discipline, Access equity, Contract leverage, Plan design fit, Workforce alignment, Data readiness | **Portfolio** | The Northrock report, section 08. |

Two tests decided it.

*Can it be produced from what we collect?* The free form asks who the report is
about, covered subscribers, funding, states of operation, current programs,
vendors, and carrier/TPA. Against that, **Set A scores 1 of 8** — only vendor
stability, and only from public research. Evidence cannot be graded without
reading the study; utilization cannot be graded without utilization. The home
page promises a portfolio scored on eight dimensions and the free report is
structurally incapable of delivering seven of them. Set B scores 8 of 8 because
the pipeline infers them from outside research. Set C scores about 2 of 8 from
the current form, but all eight are askable.

*Who is the score about?* Set B contains `cfoEngagement`, `decisionMaturity`
and `appreciationValue`, inferred from LinkedIn signals and job postings. That
is `axionia_research_agent.jsx` heritage and that tool scored companies we were
**prospecting**. CFO engagement is a sales qualification signal; it is a strange
thing to hand a client about themselves. Set B is a prospecting rubric wearing
a client-deliverable costume. Every one of Set C's axes is something a client
can act on.

**The decision: three layers, three names, no collisions.**

| Layer | Set | Lives |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect fit — internal | B, renamed | `research_schema`, admin only. Never shown to a client. It is good at this job. |
| **Portfolio score** — client-facing headline | **C** | Free scorer, home radar, report section 08 |
| Program score — the paid detail | A's axes, renamed | Per-program heatmap: report section 03, buyer deck |

Set A's axes are not discarded. Evidence, population fit and vendor stability
are exactly right for grading one vendor, which is what the buyer deck heatmap
and the report's program ledger already do. The error was never the axes; it
was calling a program scorecard a portfolio score.

*What would change our mind:* if the free form cannot be made to answer Set C
without becoming long enough to depress completion. The three questions it
needs — renewal dates, shift structure, whether anything has been independently
verified — are also the three best sales qualifiers we could add, so the
downside case is narrow. If completion drops materially after they are added,
the honest fallback is a portfolio score on fewer than eight axes that says
which ones it could not assess and why, rather than a fuller score we cannot
support.

**Scope.** Cheaper than first assumed — there is no migration, because Set A
was never persisted.

- `components/RadarPreview.tsx` — the `AXES` array and its read-out copy. Small.
- `components/ReportDemo.tsx` — axes, plus the quarter-over-quarter narrative,
  which is written against Set A's axes and is a genuine rewrite rather than a
  rename. **The real cost of this item.**
- `app/methodology/page.tsx` — see E8.
- Free scorer output and the score-report email template.
- `app/request-report/page.tsx` — three new questions.
- `components/deck/DeckFlow.tsx` and the buyer deck heatmap — confirm they are

- Nothing in `supabase/`. Set B stays exactly where it is and only stops being
  described as the client score.

This decision belongs in `PROJECT_STATE.md` under *The argument* once its
current uncommitted edits are resolved; recorded here in the meantime.

## Track F — the population exposure model

Spec at `docs/EXPOSURE_MODEL.md`, drafted 2026-08-27. Nothing built.

The segment library carries labour-market dimensions and no epidemiological
ones, so every population-fit claim on the site currently rests on one opaque
per-industry transfer rate in `ReportDemo` with no derivation and no source.

The spec replaces it with a product of named multipliers against a stated
reference population, each index at 1.00 by default, each traceable to a public
annual source — BLS CPS for age and sex mix, BLS SOII for musculoskeletal and
exposure rates by NAICS, BLS ORS or O*NET work context for chemical and
ergonomic load, BLS shift data for addressability.

Three things in it are worth not losing:

- **Covered lives is an input, not a coefficient.** Disney removed spousal
  coverage for spouses with their own employer's plan on 24 August 2026. A
  segment constant would have been wrong about them four days ago.
- **The model has a known failure direction and says so.** Prevalence and spend
  can move in opposite directions — a manufacturing workforce with more births
  than a law firm that spends more on fertility. A prevalence-only model is
  predictably wrong there, so care-seeking pathway is recorded as unmodelled
  and the example lives in the copy rather than in a coefficient.
- **A falsification test, set before the first engagement.** Segment-adjusted
  predictions must beat a flat all-industry baseline out of sample, or the
  model is decoration and gets deleted rather than tuned.

It also absorbs the fifth taxonomy problem: `ReportDemo`'s four workforce
profiles are unrelated to the library's nine segments. The demo's private
taxonomy gets deleted, not extended.
