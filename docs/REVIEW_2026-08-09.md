# Axionia — full review, 9 August 2026

Top-to-bottom pass over the repo, the schema, the deployed surface and the docs.
Written to be acted on, not filed. Ordered by when it needs your attention.

**Headline:** the codebase is in better shape than most products with a paying
customer, and the risks are not where you'd expect. Nothing here is on fire. The
two things that could actually cost you something are (1) three migrations
written today that aren't in the database yet, and (2) the fact that no email
has left the building since Resend went unconfigured — which is invisible until
the first time it matters, and the first time it matters is a customer.

## 1. Security

### What's genuinely right

Worth stating, because it's unusual and it means the rest of this list is short.

- **RLS is on for every table.** All 15 created by migrations, plus the four base
  tables (`profiles`, `intake_responses`, `reports`, `leads`) in `schema.sql`.
  There is no table in `public` without it. I checked each one individually
  rather than assuming.
- **The client report page reads through the anon client carrying the user's
  session**, so `reports_select_company_ready` does the authorisation in the
  database. Most teams reach for the service role and check `status === 'ready'`
  in TypeScript, which moves the guarantee into whichever branch someone edits
  next. You didn't.
- **Service role never reaches a client component.** Verified across every file
  carrying `"use client"`.
- **Secrets fail closed.** `if (!s || s.length < 24) return null` — a missing or
  weak signing secret disables link minting rather than minting weak links. The
  failure mode is "downloads unavailable", not "forgeable token".
- **No IP is stored anywhere**, deliberately, with the reasoning recorded in
  migration 012 rather than in someone's memory.
- **`alter default privileges`** in migration 009 means tables created by 024,
  025 and 026 inherit service-role grants automatically. I expected this to be
  the blocker and it isn't.

### What to actually fix

**S1 — `requestDeckDownload` is an unauthenticated, unthrottled write path.**
`/request-report` and `/signup` are protected by Turnstile via Supabase's own
captcha verification. The deck print gate isn't. It writes a row to `leads` and
a row to `deck_events` per submission, with no captcha and no rate limit. A
bored person with curl can fill your lead table. Severity is low — it's noise,
not exposure — but `leads` is a list you work, and a poisoned list is a list you
stop trusting. Same applies to `/api/track`. *Fix: reuse the Turnstile component
on the gate, or a simple per-address cooldown. Half an hour.*

**S2 — staff account compromise is total, and auth is email-OTP.** Anyone
holding your inbox holds `owner`, which includes role assignment. There's no
second factor and no session policy. This is the highest-severity item on the
list by a distance, and it's a Supabase Auth setting rather than code. *Fix:
enable MFA on your own account this week.*

**S3 — `contacts.notes` and `contacts.source` are internal writing about named
people.** RLS is service-role-only so nothing leaks to a client. But `/privacy`
publishes `privacy@axionia.com` as the address for access and deletion requests,
and a subject access request would reach these fields. Write what you'd be
content to read aloud. Not a defect — a habit to adopt now, while there are
twelve rows and not twelve hundred.

**S4 — `privacy@axionia.com` may not exist.** `/privacy` commits to it publicly.
An unrouted mailbox on a privacy page is worse than no address, and this is
already flagged in `PROJECT_STATE.md`. *Fix: five minutes in your mail provider.*

**S5 — the privacy policy hasn't been through counsel.** Fine today. It stops
being fine the moment a customer contract references it, which is the moment
you're closest to signing one.

**S6 — bearer-token URLs, accepted rather than solved.** `?dl=`, `?k=` and now
`?v=` are all forwardable. Mitigations exist and are good: watermarks carry the
recipient's name, the print gate now asks who is actually printing, and `?v=`
serves approved versions only. But the founders deck at $250K has **no per-link
revoke** — rotating `DECK_LINK_SECRET` kills every link at once. Known and
documented. Worth a per-link revoke before that deck goes to a wider list.

## 2. Out of sync

**O1 — three migrations are written and not applied.** 024 (firms), 025
(contacts, steps) and 026 (deck versions) exist in the repo and not in Supabase.
The company hub queries them. Until they run, those panels render empty states
and the page is quietly less than it looks. **This is today's job.**

**O2 — there are now two buyer decks, and they disagree.** `components/deck/
slides.tsx` is the live `/deck` route and opens *"Fifteen people reviewed it.
None of them checked it."* The standalone `axionia_buyer_deck.html` in project
knowledge — the one we reworked this morning — opens *"The decisions are big.
The tools to evaluate them shouldn't be a black box."* Two artifacts, one name,
divergent arguments. Pick one as canonical. My view: the React deck is the one
with tracking, tailoring, print watermarks and an approval gate, so it wins, and
this morning's copy work should be ported into it.

**O3 — `next_action` and `company_steps` both exist.** The steps list supersedes
the single next action, but the column is still read by `CrmPanel` and the
companies list view. Two answers to "what's next" is one too many. Flagged in a
comment; migrate and drop when you're confident.

**O4 — `STAGES` is defined twice.** `lib/crm.ts` holds objects with labels and
notes; `app/admin/companies/actions.ts` holds a plain string array for
validation. Add a stage to the first and the server silently rejects it. Not a
bug today, a bug the day you add a stage.

**O5 — the research module README is stale.** It says step 4 is outstanding and
that "a released free report has nothing for the client to open." Both
`/reports/[id]` and the dashboard listing exist now. A README that under-reports
what's built costs you the confidence to sell it.

**O6 — project knowledge is a snapshot, and `PROJECT_STATE.md` already says the
repo wins.** That's the right rule. It means the deck, brand and spec HTML files
in project knowledge should be treated as history, not source.

## 3. When the first customer signs

The honest finding: **almost nothing here is a code gap.** The product side is
ready. What's missing is operational, and per your own ethos most of it should
stay out of the codebase.

### Real gaps

| Gap | Why it bites | Code? |
|---|---|---|
| **Resend unconfigured** | Every send is skipped and written to `email_log` as `status = 'skipped'`. The release email — the one that tells a paying customer their report exists — silently does not arrive. | No. Config. |
| **No invoicing path** | No Stripe anywhere in the repo. | No, and don't build one. |
| **No MSA / SOW** | Nothing to sign. | No. A document. |
| **No engagement runbook** | What happens between "yes" and "report delivered" lives in your head. | No. |
| **Paid tier is unbuilt** | Entitlement checks, artifact ingestion, module registry — all listed as not built. | Yes, eventually. Not for customer one. |
| **No deletion process** | `/privacy` promises it. | No. A runbook. |

### Implementation plan — deliberately small

Customer one should be served the way you already work, with three things fixed
and nothing built.

1. **Set `RESEND_API_KEY`** and send yourself a release email end to end. Confirm
   `email_log` flips from `skipped` to sent. *~20 minutes, `docs/EMAIL-SETUP.md`
   has the steps.*
2. **Route `privacy@axionia.com`** to your inbox. *5 minutes.*
3. **Write a one-page SOW template** — scope, fee, dates, what you deliver, what
   you need from them, the independence statement from `/research`. A document,
   not a feature. *An hour.*
4. **Invoice from the Stripe dashboard by hand.** At one customer, and at ten, a
   billing integration is a liability with a monthly maintenance cost. Revisit at
   twenty.
5. **Use what you already built to run the account.** `stage = 'client'`, the
   brief, the steps list, contacts. That is a CRM. Don't add to it until it
   demonstrably fails.

The test for anything else: *would not having this have cost me the customer?*
If no, it waits.
