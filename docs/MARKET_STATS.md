# Market statistics — source of record

Every number that appears on the public site, in a deck, or in a report must
trace to a row in this file. `FINANCIAL_MODEL.md` does this for the company's
own numbers; this does it for the market's.

**Rules.**

- Source, publication date and retrieval date on every row. A number without a
  retrieval date cannot be aged, and a number that cannot be aged gets stale
  silently on a page nobody is re-reading.
- Prefer the primary release over coverage of it. Where only coverage was
  reachable, the row says so.
- Trend figures are worthless without knowing whether they are **before or
  after plan design changes**. Every row states which. Most published
  disagreements between surveys are this distinction, not a real disagreement.
- If a number is our own view rather than a finding, it does not belong here.
  Put it in the copy as a view and let it stand as one.

## Checked and rejected

Recorded so they are not reintroduced.

- **"Medical trend for 2026/2027 is 13%."** No survey supports this. The 13%
  figure is **medical stop-loss premium** (Segal, Q3 2026), which is a
  different thing and a better one for our argument. Medical trend is 9–9.5%
  before plan changes.
- **"Cost per employee has gone from $2,000 to $20,000 in ten years."** Not
  supported. Aon's series runs $16,212 (2025) to $17,562 (2026) to $19,000+
  (2027 projected); ten years ago the figure was roughly $12,000. The real
  shape is about +50% over a decade. Use the Mercer 3%-to-double-digits framing
  instead, which makes the same point and survives challenge.
- **"Retention and attraction will shift materially over the next five years."**
  No authoritative source found; searches return content marketing. This is our
  view and should be published as a view, with reasoning, not dressed as a
  finding. See the talent thesis in the copy.
